## Review Method

This review combines:

- Live browser inspection of Seedwake Meadow, River Gardens, Fossil Canyon, Forge Settlement, Glass Marsh, Storm Coast, Lantern Forest, and Star Reach.
- Phone inspection at 390 x 844 of the game, map, Den, Trading Post, ceremony, and accessible 2D path.
- Wrong-answer recovery and active-choice stability checks.
- Code inspection of the pixel runtime, physical task expansion, verb handlers, music routing, accessibility parity, progression, mastery, rewards, telemetry, and reporting.
- Generated analysis of every stop's physical response count and opening prompt.
- The current 555-test release suite, twenty-nine dedicated browser accessibility/reflow/journey/animation/mobile-surface/network-recovery tests, and production build evidence.

## Verdict

Sound Seekers is now a credible and unusually well-instrumented educational game prototype. It is not yet a premium 10/10 game.

The strongest work is below the surface: decodability, mastery integrity, correction, review scheduling, persistence, cumulative equipment, accessible parity, telemetry, and reporting. The weakest work is the part the child actually feels: physical control variety, authored level composition, character performance, animation, visual consistency, pacing, sound identity, and moment-to-moment narrative consequence.

**Overall premium-game score: 5.3/10**  
**Educational systems score: 7.8/10**  
**Production release readiness: 4.5/10**

The game should remain labelled preview outside Seedwake. A green test suite proves that the systems are coherent. It does not prove that the experience is delightful, legible on every device, or ready for a child audience.

## Release-Blocking Findings

### P0. Phone layouts can hide a required answer

At 390 x 844, the left answer in Glass Marsh s21 was visibly clipped beyond the viewport. River Gardens s7 reproduced the same problem. This is not cosmetic: an answer can be hard to inspect or reach in the primary world view.

The runtime positions choices around the resident without applying a camera-safe viewport correction at `questPixelRuntime.js:1198-1221`. The camera zoom changes for phones, but staging does not reflow around the usable rectangle.

**10/10 result:** Every choice silhouette and label stays fully visible with a minimum safe margin on supported phones and tablets, including long graphemes such as `ure`, `eigh`, and `tion`.

### P0. Audio-led tasks can expose a silent replay button

Sixteen taught targets still have no approved recording: `aw`, `ore`, `air`, `are`, `ear`, `ure`, `le`, `tion`, `y_ie`, `y_ee`, `oo_short`, `ow_ou`, `c_s`, `g_j`, `ch_k`, and `ea_e` in `questSequence.js:42-49`.

The pixel HUD renders the replay button whenever an encounter is active at `QuestPixelWorld.jsx:503-521`; it does not check whether the requested clip exists. The instruction may still say "Find the letter that matches the sound" even when replay produces silence.

**10/10 result:** No audio-dependent task is generated without an approved clip, and no replay control is shown unless it can play. Silent targets must use a genuinely visual or word-context task until the final voice set exists.

### P1. Named verbs do not yet create enough different play

The 35 later-chapter verbs are composed from only four recipe families: 13 single, 4 delivery, 9 assembly, and 9 rhythm. The recipes provide valuable state and content structure, but the pixel renderer still creates choices through one staging function and resolves them through one collision loop at `questPixelRuntime.js:1198-1221` and `questPixelRuntime.js:1384-1400`.

Later verbs also receive the default movement/camera profile because only the five Seedwake mechanics have explicit profiles at `questSliceSystems.js:220-236`.

**10/10 result:** Fishing, climbing, brushing fossils, steering a ferry, turning an observatory, fitting a lens, signalling a fleet, and following a constellation must have observably different input timing, motion, camera, animation, failure, and world consequence. A muted five-second clip should identify the verb.

### P1. The physical action budget breaks the intended pacing contract

The older release test counts curriculum beats and keeps them at eight or fewer. The rendered physical-task expansion can multiply one beat into multiple pick, carry, fit, and rhythm stages.

Across all 40 current stops:

- Minimum physical actions: 3
- Maximum physical actions: 26
- Average physical actions: 10.8
- s13 requires 25 actions
- s15 requires 26 actions

There are 433 generated physical stages in the complete journey. Twenty-two of 40 opening tasks use the exact prompt "Find the letter that matches the sound".

**10/10 result:** Count the actions the child actually performs, not the source beats. Cap a normal stop at 6-10 meaningful actions and one encounter at five. Move extra evidence into later spaced review.

**Status after Pass 88:** Resolved locally. The release gate now measures all 40 rendered stops at 4-8 actions, 6.72 on average, with no more than three maximum-load stops in sequence. External child fatigue and repeated-play evidence remain required for a 10.

### P1. Carry visibility has a zero-index bug

`questPixelRuntime.js:1258-1269` tests `carryFromStage` by truthiness. A valid first carry source is stage `0`, so later delivery and assembly actions whose second action is not literally named `carry` or `place-plank` can fail to render the held object.

**10/10 result:** Every pick-up visibly attaches the correct object to the Beastie until a destination accepts it, across all delivery and assembly recipes and after checkpoint restore.

## Major Experience Findings

### Art and world composition

- Chapter colour grades and set pieces are now distinguishable.
- The repeated camera composition is still resident above, Beastie below, and three labels between them.
- The same bushes, rocks, path blocks, resident scale, shadows, and object glow recur across incompatible worlds.
- Large procedural path and water patches sit beside 16 x 16 character sheets and crisp runtime text, producing mixed pixel density.
- Fossil bones, Forge gears, Marsh signs, Storm signs, and Star tokens read as decorated answer plates more than physical world objects.
- Several letter labels overrun or visually fight their silhouettes, especially advanced graphemes.
- The worlds lack authored negative space, landmarks that guide navigation, interiors, secrets, local micro-stories, and cinematic reveals.

**Required result:** Build hand-composed tile maps, not profiles painted onto one road generator. Define one pixel grid, outline rule, palette budget, scale hierarchy, shadow rule, and typography treatment for everything.

### Character performance

- The Beastie preserves appearance and cumulative gear, which is a real strength.
- It has four directions and four walk frames per direction, but no convincing run, stop, turn, pick-up, carry, push, jump, celebrate, fail, or interact set.
- Residents play four walk frames forward and backward as their idle animation at `questPixelRuntime.js:595-612`.
- Residents are placed by encounter order and rotate through small world pools at `questPixelRuntime.js:1067-1085`.
- Characters do not walk into scenes, face the task reliably, exchange objects, react to weather, or perform chapter-specific work.

**Required result:** Each core character needs idle, four-direction locomotion, anticipation, action, success, concern, and celebration. Each chapter needs a bespoke cast with task-specific performances.

### Movement and camera

- Left and right are now correct.
- Movement applies a fixed velocity immediately and stops immediately at `questPixelRuntime.js:1320-1367`.
- The player is clamped to a narrow calculated corridor and a forward barrier, so apparent free roam is limited.
- Pointer travel can take too long and can leave the Beastie near an edge while the camera prioritises the resident.
- Every encounter uses almost the same zoom and focus behaviour.

**Required result:** Add acceleration, deceleration, turn anticipation, pathfinding or reliable steering, local collision, obstacle navigation, verb-specific camera language, and phone-safe camera bounds.

### UI and child experience

- The immediate objective is much clearer than earlier builds.
- The prompt panel occupies a large amount of the playfield and reinforces the feeling of a quiz overlay.
- The collectible tally is an icon and number with no immediate explanation of why the item matters.
- The phone Den truncates the destination name.
- The phone map's horizontal chapter navigation clips labels.
- The phone Trading Post category tabs appear as unlabeled dark slivers, the creature preview crops at the top, and repeated thumbnails make small equipment differences hard to compare.
- The reward ceremony is polished, but it is still a modal over a blurred world rather than an in-world event the child causes.

**Required result:** Treat the HUD as quiet status, stage tasks through character animation and world affordance, make every reward's use obvious at collection time, and pass a complete 320-430 px phone matrix.

### Educational quality

The underlying educational architecture is excellent:

- Words remain decodable at the point they appear.
- Mastery requires repeated evidence across sessions and shells.
- Cramming does not create false mastery.
- Misses drive correction and future review.
- Accessible 2D uses the same task and evidence model.
- Teacher reporting distinguishes journey completion from actual mastery.

The child-facing weakness is that educational evidence is too often expressed through repeated label selection. The game needs more listening in context, oral blending, segmenting, word building, meaningful reading, and comprehension embedded in world action without increasing total response load.

### Music and sound

- Seedwake has travel, action, and ceremony arrangements with correct ducking under speech.
- Only five quest MP3 themes exist, encoded at 22.05 kHz, 96 kbps, mono.
- River, Forge, Storm, Lantern, and Star identities currently point to tracks created for other arcade games at `gameMusic.js:72-105`.
- Chapter ambience strings describe frogs, surf, ropes, moths, glass, wind, and machines, but the pixel game does not yet have an authored adaptive ambience system matching those promises.
- Generic Seedwake success, wrong, magic, and pickup sounds are reused throughout the world.

**Required result:** Compose a coherent soundtrack with chapter stems, traversal/action/ceremony states, location ambience, verb-specific Foley, character vocalisations, and approved phonics voice. Validate loudness and intelligibility on tablet speakers.

### Rewards, narrative, and motivation

- Sparks are real and cannot be duplicated by sync.
- Relics have abilities and cumulative gear works.
- The Stone Wall honestly separates mastery from route completion.
- The chapter ceremony has strong hierarchy and a satisfying first reveal.

However:

- Ordinary collectibles have little visible world consequence.
- Chapter conflicts and objectives live mainly on the map rather than during play.
- Residents rarely behave like characters with needs, relationships, memory, or change.
- Repaired landmarks do not yet create enough new traversal, spectacle, or future utility.
- The long-term economy is clearer to the code than to the child.

**Required result:** Every pickup should feed a visible short-term goal; every stop should change a place or character; every chapter relic should unlock a repeatedly useful action; later visits should show that the world remembers.

### Accessibility, reliability, and performance

- The accessible 2D path is clear, calm, and in some respects more visually coherent than pixel mode.
- Semantic answer buttons, keyboard shortcuts, reduced motion, high contrast, checkpointing, renderer fallback, and error handling are valuable production foundations.
- The 477 tests and deterministic builders make regressions easier to catch.

Remaining gaps:

- No formal screen-reader, switch-control, 200 percent zoom, colour-vision, or school-device session is certified.
- No observed child session validates prompt comprehension or touch accuracy.
- The lazy Phaser bundle is about 1.439 MB minified and 377.54 KB gzip before scene assets.
- No 20-minute production-device soak proves stable memory, input latency, or frame pacing across all 40 stops.

## Sequence To Reach 10/10

1. Fix phone-safe choice staging, silent replay controls, and carry stage `0` immediately.
2. Replace the beat-count release gate with a physical-action budget and reduce every stop above 10 actions.
3. Build five truly different Seedwake control grammars in pixel mode, including bespoke movement, camera, animation, failure, and consequence.
4. Hand-compose and certify the five Seedwake maps. Stop expanding later content until this chapter is fun in repeated child play.
5. Produce one coherent Seedwake character and animation kit for Beastie, guide, residents, task objects, and reactions.
6. Replace procedural signs and text-on-shapes with authored physical object interactions.
7. Rebuild the phone HUD, map tabs, Den destination card, and Trading Post from a 320 px minimum width upward.
8. Finish the approved voice set, create chapter soundscapes, and commission adaptive music stems.
9. Make collectibles, repairs, relics, and return visits visibly change play.
10. Run repeated child sessions and physical iPad/Chromebook tests; revise from observed confusion, boredom, and delight.
11. Promote River Gardens only after Seedwake meets every gate, then repeat one chapter at a time.
12. Make pixel mode the default only after every enabled chapter has equivalent proof.

## Definition Of 10/10

Sound Seekers reaches 10/10 only when all of the following are simultaneously true:

- A child understands every task from staging and sound without adult explanation.
- Every chapter is recognisable from a silent five-second clip.
- Every signature verb feels mechanically and visually distinct.
- No clean stop exceeds the tested action budget.
- No answer, label, control, or reward clips on supported devices.
- Every required phonics cue has an approved recording.
- Characters perform, react, remember, and visibly change the world.
- The complete game survives production-device soaks without input or frame degradation.
- Accessible play preserves the same learning evidence and emotional reward.
- Children voluntarily ask to continue, and observed sessions show learning rather than interface struggle.

Until those results are observed, 10/10 remains the acceptance standard, not the score.
