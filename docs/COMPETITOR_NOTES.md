# Competitor Notes — LiteracyPath

Research date: July 2026. Focus: patterns implementable WITHOUT new artwork — timing, flow, feedback, copy, structure, celebration cadence, progression visibility. Sources cited inline.

Competitors covered: Raz-Kids / Kids A-Z, Teach Your Monster to Read, Starfall, Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo ABC.

---

## 1. Raz-Kids / Kids A-Z (Learning A-Z)

### (a) Child home screen
- Students land in the Kids A-Z student portal after logging in — a hub showing their assignments, the leveled "Reading Room" bookroom (400+ ebooks), their star total, and entry points to the reward areas ([Student Portal](https://www.raz-plus.com/helpful-tools/student-portal/), [Common Sense review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).
- Teacher assignments surface directly in the portal on the start date the teacher set — the child sees "what's for me today" without hunting ([Assignments Feature](https://www.raz-plus.com/technology/assignments-feature/)).
- Common Sense notes the design is "aging" and "isn't quite up to par with some slicker competitors" — a leveled library alone doesn't carry engagement ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).

### (b) Lesson/level maps and progression
- Progression is the reading level ladder (aa–Z): read/listen → record yourself → take the quiz → pass quizzes to level up to more complex texts ([Get Students Reading](https://www.raz-kids.com/main/viewpage/name/get-students-reading/), [Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).
- Teachers place students via a Reading Placement Tool or pick the level manually; students then "progress to increasingly complex texts as they read books and pass quizzes" ([Learning A-Z parent guide](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/11174404-getting-started-with-learning-a-z-a-guide-for-parents)).
- Every book has the same 3-step loop (listen / read / quiz) — predictable structure children learn once and repeat.

### (c) Reward loops and celebration
- Stars are earned for EVERY activity across all Learning A-Z products, not just big milestones ([Student Incentives & Awards](https://www.raz-plus.com/technology/student-incentive-awards/)).
- Stars are spent in the "Star Zone" on two customization sandboxes: the Raz Rocket (decorate a spaceship room with aliens, furniture, robots) and the Avatar Builder ([Kids A-Z Student Incentive Program](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/8103068-kids-a-z-student-incentive-program)).
- Notable onboarding trick: **all students receive a 500-star bonus when they first enter the Avatar Builder** — the reward economy starts warm, never at zero ([Incentive Program help article](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/8103068-kids-a-z-student-incentive-program)).
- Badges layer on top of stars for milestone achievements, framed explicitly as goal-setting ([Student Incentives](https://www.raz-plus.com/technology/student-incentive-awards/)).
- Incentives can be toggled on/off per class by the teacher ([Learning A-Z help](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/11174404-getting-started-with-learning-a-z-a-guide-for-parents)).

### (d) Game feedback for young children
- Feedback is quiz-centric (multiple choice + one constructed response per book) rather than per-interaction — the weakest part of the product for 4–7s ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).
- The listen-first option (hear the book before reading it) is a scaffold that lowers frustration for emergent readers ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).

### (e) Session flow (open → learning)
- Login is the friction point: teacher username → class chart of student names → icon/picture password for pre-readers ([Student Login Instructions](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/7025447-student-login-instructions)). Teachers print login cards to cope ([printable login cards guide](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1657744403/basdk12org/ma95nudjmuahzcaz84if/printingreadinga-zstudentlogincards.pdf)).
- Once in, assignments-first layout means the child's next task is one tap away. 24/7 home access is a selling point ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).

### (f) Teacher dashboard/reporting
- The strongest teacher offering of the five. From the kidsa-z.com dashboard: class roster management, weekly analytics, a Class Skill Report mapped to standards/reading skills, and per-student Activity Reports ([OUSD Teacher Central summary](https://teachercentral.ousd.org/tech/online-programs/raz-kids), [Teacher Resources on Raz-Kids](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/7025500-teacher-resources-on-raz-kids)).
- Assign any resource to individuals or groups with future start dates; assignments are editable in an Assignments area ([Assigning Resources](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/7025844-assigning-resources-on-raz-kids)).
- Teachers can LISTEN to students' recorded read-alouds for fluency/running records — "a running record from every student with 5-10 minutes of effort" ([Common Sense teacher review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/raz-kids)).

## 2. Teach Your Monster to Read

### (a) Child home screen
- There is effectively no home screen — the child logs in and is immediately inside the game narrative: an alien monster whose spaceship crashed, with the island map as the hub ([Common Sense review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)).
- The monster avatar (built by the child before the first lesson — eyes, horns, colors) IS the identity layer; everything earned attaches to it ([Smarter Learning Guide review](https://smarterlearningguide.com/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)).

### (b) Lesson/level maps and progression
- Mini-games are laid out across islands on a colorful map "that visually tracks kids' progress" — First Steps has 8 islands, Fun With Words has 7 villages; complete an island to unlock the next ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read), [Classwork overview](https://classwork.com/teach-your-monster-to-read/)).
- Progression is wrapped in plot: each game collects story-relevant items (e.g. spaceship parts) so finishing a world means the ship flies to the next one — children "loved progressing through the lands, building their ship and flying to a new planet" ([Common Sense teacher review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)).
- Three sequenced games (First Steps → Fun With Words → Champion Reader) map to phonics phases ([TYM overview](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/teach-your-monster-to-read-overview/)).

### (c) Reward loops and celebration
- **Reward cadence is per-grapheme, with choice**: "an interactive rewards system providing a choice of accessories or virtual treats for their monster every time they have learned a new grapheme and when a 'world' has been completed" ([Smarter Learning Guide](https://smarterlearningguide.com/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)).
- Won accessories "immediately appear on their bodies" — zero delay between earning and wearing ([Phonics.org review](https://www.phonics.org/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)).
- Stars are also collected ambiently while moving around the map ([Classwork](https://classwork.com/teach-your-monster-to-read/)).
- Certificates "brought to life with animation" celebrate completion of major stages ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)).
- Rewards are deliberately silly (winning underwear for your monster) — humor is part of the reward value ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)).

### (d) Game feedback for young children
- Immediate feedback with adaptive scaffolding, tuned to avoid guessing: "just enough intuitive feedback that children can figure it out almost entirely on their own, but not so much that it encourages guessing" ([Modulo review](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/teachyourmonstertoread), [Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)).
- **Error-driven repetition**: "If they have trouble with a specific part of the game, those sounds appear more frequently later on, so they get more chances to practice" ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)).
- Weakness to avoid copying: games don't always explain the goal, so kids sometimes need adult help on first play ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)).

### (e) Session flow (open → learning)
- Class Mode removes login entirely: teacher enables a classcode, players skip the login process and pick their name ([Class Mode setup](https://help.teachyourmonster.org/en/articles/5458692-setting-up-class-mode-in-teach-your-monster-to-read)).
- Designed for "regular, short sessions" — teachers use it in 5-minute bursts up to ~20-minute sessions ([Using the game in school](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/monster-news/using-the-game-in-school)).
- Because the map remembers position, resuming = tap name → land on map → tap the next pulsing island. No menu diving.

### (f) Teacher dashboard/reporting
- Free teacher dashboard with class roster upload; a Stats tab shows **strongest and weakest grapheme for the whole class** plus a graph of class performance per grapheme ([TYM Stats](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/teachers/helpful-articles-for-teachers/teach-your-monster-stats/)).
- Per-student: a list of every grapheme completed with accuracy; results exportable to Excel ([TYM Stats](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/teachers/helpful-articles-for-teachers/teach-your-monster-stats/)).

## 3. Starfall

### (a) Child home screen
- A browsable grid of content areas (ABCs, Learn to Read, math, songs) with no login required for free content; "bright, colorful and kid-centric" with icon-driven navigation that pre-readers can operate ([Common Sense review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/starfall), [Education World review](https://www.educationworld.com/awards/2003/r0303-18.shtml)).
- Criticism: "a lot of mouse navigation to get through the site" — deep menus challenge the youngest users ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/starfall)). Accessibility options (large cursor, keyboard controls, screen reader compatibility) are a differentiator ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/starfall)).

### (b) Lesson/level maps and progression
- Content is sequenced pedagogically (systematic sequential phonics; the 15-book Learn to Read series with vowel-pal characters like Zac the Rat and Peg the Hen) but there is **no enforced path and no per-child progression state** — it's free exploration ([Starfall Learn to Read](https://www.starfall.com/h/ltr-classic/), [Cathy Duffy review](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/phonics-reading/phonics-reading-programs/starfall-reading-resources)).
- The sequence lives in teacher guides ([teach.starfall.com](https://teach.starfall.com/guides/kindergarten/reading)), not in the child UI.

### (c) Reward loops and celebration
- Essentially none: motivation comes "through exploration, positive reinforcement, and play" itself; encouragement plays on completing activities but there is no currency, no collection, no streak ([Educational App Store review](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/starfall-learn-to-read)).
- Users explicitly ask for one: "a reward system... (tickets to exchange for little online prizes) would make Starfall even better and would give kids the extra incentive to push a bit harder" ([Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/starfall-learn-to-read)).

### (d) Game feedback for young children
- Heavy audiovisual interactivity: every clickable element speaks/animates, which works well for emergent readers, ELL students, and children with special needs ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/starfall)).
- Feedback is per-interaction and always gentle; no fail states, no scores.

### (e) Session flow (open → learning)
- Fastest cold-start of the five: no account, no login — open the site/app and one tap starts an activity ([Starfall Learn to Read app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/starfall-learn-to-read/id551817261)).
- Trade-off: with no state and no "next up" guidance, the child (or an adult) must choose what to do, and nothing accumulates between sessions.

### (f) Teacher dashboard/reporting
- Official position: "individualized tracking and reports are not provided by Starfall"; only aggregate usage reports on request for funding purposes ([Starfall help: tracking](https://help.starfall.com/help/about-student-tracking-and-reports)).
- Teacher value is delivered as offline material instead: lesson plans, guides, and printables in the Parent-Teacher Center ([teach.starfall.com](https://teach.starfall.com/)).

## 4. Khan Academy Kids

### (a) Child home screen
- Home screen = one **big green play button** that starts the personalized Learning Path with Kodi the bear, plus a Library and "character rooms" for free-choice browsing ([Khan Kids: Learning Topics](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014856151-Learning-Topics-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-in-Educational-Settings)).
- Subject areas are personified: Ollo the elephant owns phonics/letter sounds, Reya the red panda owns storytime/writing, Peck the hummingbird owns numbers, Sandy the dingo owns puzzles/logic — the character IS the navigation label, so pre-readers know where things live ([Learning Topics](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014856151-Learning-Topics-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-in-Educational-Settings)).

### (b) Lesson/level maps and progression
- The Learning Path is adaptive and mastery-based: it "automatically cycles through topics" and "surfaces content at a level that is appropriate for each student based on how they are doing in the app"; strong students skip ahead, struggling students get more practice lessons ([Learn more about the Learning Path](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048828572-Learn-more-about-the-Learning-Path), [How the learning level adjusts](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041615571-How-does-the-learning-level-adjust-and-how-do-I-view-my-child-s-progress)).
- Every lesson exists in four difficulty variants — Main, Practice 1, Practice 2, Basic — so differentiation is a label swap, not new content ([Module 3: Assigning lessons](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042194831-Module-3-Assigning-lessons)).

### (c) Reward loops and celebration
- Deliberately low-key: "After completing activities, kids choose a prize to add to their animal friends' collections" — again reward-with-choice, and the collection belongs to the characters, reinforcing the relationship ([Common Sense Media review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/khan-academy-kids)).
- No streaks, no leaderboards — the app leans on character warmth and variety instead.

### (d) Game feedback for young children
- Kodi narrates and guides throughout; characters deliver verbal encouragement on every activity; everything is read aloud so non-readers are never stuck ([Modulo review](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/khan-academy-kids-review)).

### (e) Session flow (open → learning)
- Best-in-class: open app → tap the big green button → learning starts. Zero decisions required from the child ([Learning Topics](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014856151-Learning-Topics-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-in-Educational-Settings)).
- **Bounded sessions**: "The app encourages kids to put their device down after an age-appropriate learning journey, which is between 5 to 20 minutes per day" — the session has a designed ending, not an infinite scroll ([Parent guide](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006764812-Parent-Guide-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-at-Home)).

### (f) Teacher dashboard/reporting
- Class Accounts with a web Teacher Dashboard ([NEW: Teacher Dashboard on the web](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/14738508196379--NEW-Teacher-Dashboard-on-the-web)); assign lessons to whole class, small group, or individual; find lessons by Library browse, from the Class Report, or by standards search ([All about Teacher Tools](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041862972-All-about-Teacher-Tools-in-Khan-Academy-Kids)).
- Two reports: Assignments (only what the teacher assigned) and All Progress (everything the child did, including Learning Path and free-choice Library play). Tap any score to see full attempt history; email reports to yourself or to parents ([Module 4: Viewing student progress](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042197491-Module-4-Viewing-student-progress)).

## 5. Duolingo ABC

### (a) Child home screen
- The home screen is the progression map itself: "each Level is represented by a building, and within each building, lessons are represented by steps on a learning path" — child taps the next glowing stop ([Common Sense Media review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)).
- The whole app is framed as a mission: "help Duo the green owl collect books" — the books collected along the path form the child's library ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read), [Phonics.org review](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).

### (b) Lesson/level maps and progression
- 10 themed levels, 300+ bite-sized lessons averaging ~5 minutes each; strictly linear — "each step along the path is purposeful as new games review what's already been introduced and then builds further" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/id1440502568), [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)).
- **Lesson goals are stated in plain language at the start of every level**; the scope & sequence is published for adults ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/), [scope & sequence PDF](https://lit-lessons-cdn.duolingo.com/resources/duolingo_abc_scope_and_sequence_english.pdf)).
- Parents can set the starting point and lock levels to prevent skipping ahead ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).

### (c) Reward loops and celebration
- Stars for completing lessons AND stars just for returning each day; stars unlock new levels and stories ([Reviewed.app](https://reviewed.app/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/), [EducationalAppStore](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/duolingo-abc)).
- **"Proximity rewards"**: the reward (a new book for the library) is itself a reading artifact, keeping the loop on-subject rather than a separate arcade economy ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).
- Micro-rewards are social/character-based: verbal encouragement from Duo, playful beats like a pretend food fight ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).
- Parent-reported outcome of the star loop: kids who started at 1 lesson/day escalated to 5–10 lessons/day chasing stars ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/id1440502568?see-all=reviews)).

### (d) Game feedback for young children
- **Celebration on every achievement**: "Duolingo ABC uses delightful sound effects, illustrations, and animation to celebrate every achievement" — not only at lesson end ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)).
- **Mistakes are coached, not punished**: "when kids make mistakes, the app provides quick tips via voice-over and/or visual cues and encourages kids to try again" ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)).
- Letter tracing gives corrective formation feedback; Elkonin boxes used for spelling ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).
- Cautionary notes from literacy experts: voice recognition is too lenient, timed reading pressure can stress new readers, and some word-building activities use words beyond taught skills — misaligned difficulty teaches guessing ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)).

### (e) Session flow (open → learning)
- No child login; open app → tap the next path stop → learning in seconds. Lessons are ~5 minutes, so a natural session is 1–2 stops ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/id1440502568)).

### (f) Teacher dashboard/reporting
- No ABC-specific classroom dashboard: Duolingo for Schools targets the language app (and is being sunset July 31, 2027); ABC is positioned as free for families/teachers without per-student reporting ([Duolingo for Schools](https://schools.duolingo.com/), [Duolingo ABC](https://abc.duolingo.com/)).

## Top 10 gaps for LiteracyPath

Concrete, implementable without new artwork:

1. **Celebrate every correct answer, not just round ends.** Duolingo ABC "uses delightful sound effects, illustrations, and animation to celebrate every achievement" ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)). Add a ~1–2s micro-celebration (existing gold-voice praise clip + CSS pop/sparkle on the answered element) after each correct tap in every station game, with a bigger beat reserved for round end.

2. **One big "Play" button that auto-picks the next activity.** Khan Kids' home is a single green button that starts the adaptive path — zero child decisions ([Khan Kids](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014856151-Learning-Topics-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-in-Educational-Settings)). LiteracyPath's daily missions should have one oversized primary button ("Start today's quest") that deep-links straight into the first incomplete mission step, with the mission list as secondary UI.

3. **Reward moments should offer a choice.** TYM gives "a choice of accessories or virtual treats... every time they have learned a new grapheme" ([Smarter Learning Guide](https://smarterlearningguide.com/teach-your-monster-to-read-review/)); Khan Kids has kids "choose a prize" after activities ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/khan-academy-kids)). At cycle/station completion, present 2–3 existing gem/badge options and let the child tap one, instead of auto-granting.

4. **Coach mistakes with a tip + instant retry, never a fail state.** Duolingo ABC answers mistakes with "quick tips via voice-over and/or visual cues and encourages kids to try again" ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)); TYM tunes feedback so it "can't encourage guessing" ([Modulo](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/teachyourmonstertoread)). Audit every arcade/station game: wrong answer → replay the target sound + highlight the correct option briefly + same question again; no buzzer sounds, no lost points.

5. **State the learning goal in child language at the start of each cycle.** Duolingo ABC's "lesson goals are clearly stated at the beginning of each level" ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)). One gold-voice line + one text line when entering a station/cycle: "Today we're learning the /sh/ sound!" — copy only, no art.

6. **Re-surface missed sounds automatically.** In TYM, "if they have trouble with a specific part of the game, those sounds appear more frequently later on" ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read)). Track per-phoneme error rates and weight item selection in later games/missions toward the child's weak sounds.

7. **Design the session ending.** Khan Kids "encourages kids to put their device down after an age-appropriate learning journey (5 to 20 minutes)" ([Khan Kids parent guide](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006764812-Parent-Guide-Using-Khan-Academy-Kids-at-Home)). When the daily mission set completes, play a proper "quest complete" celebration screen that says today is done and previews tomorrow — a designed stopping point that builds parent trust and daily-return habit.

8. **Pay a small daily return bonus.** Duolingo ABC awards stars just for "returning each day" ([Reviewed.app](https://reviewed.app/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/)), and Raz-Kids seeds the economy with a 500-star bonus on first entry to the Avatar Builder ([Learning A-Z](https://help.learninga-z.com/en/articles/8103068-kids-a-z-student-incentive-program)). Grant a small gem bonus with a one-line gold-voice celebration on first open each day, and a warm one-time gem grant on first Treasure Den visit.

9. **Make the map show state at a glance: done / current / locked, with the next stop pulsing.** TYM's island map "visually tracks kids' progress" and Duolingo ABC renders lessons as steps with one obvious next stop ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/teach-your-monster-to-read), [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)). On the quest map, add a CSS pulse/glow on the next station, checkmark/dim treatment on completed ones, and a lock treatment on future ones — pure styling on existing markers.

10. **Give the teacher dashboard a "strongest/weakest sound" summary and assign-from-report.** TYM's Stats tab shows the class's strongest and weakest grapheme with per-student accuracy and Excel export ([TYM Stats](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/teachers/helpful-articles-for-teachers/teach-your-monster-stats/)); Khan Kids lets teachers assign directly from the Class Report and email reports to parents ([Khan Kids Teacher Tools](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041862972-All-about-Teacher-Tools-in-Khan-Academy-Kids)). Aggregate existing per-game phoneme results into a class strongest/weakest panel with a one-click "practice this sound" assignment.

**Bonus pattern worth stealing (no art needed):** Duolingo ABC's rewards are "proximity rewards" — the prize for progressing is unlocking another *book to read* ([Phonics.org](https://www.phonics.org/learn-to-read-duoling-abc-review/)). LiteracyPath's guided reading books could double as unlockable rewards on the quest map, keeping the reward loop on-subject.
