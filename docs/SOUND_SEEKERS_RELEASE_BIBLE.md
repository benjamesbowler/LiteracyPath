## Product Standard

Sound Seekers must become a premium phonics adventure whose literacy evidence is as strong as its internal mastery model. The child should experience a responsive game, not a question bank placed in a 3D scene.

The target result is:

- A five-year-old always knows the one immediate action without needing to read explanatory copy.
- Every answer object is readable, reachable, and forgiving to tap.
- Movement, camera, interaction, and chapter handoff never fight the child.
- Each chapter has a recognisable place, cast, physical verb set, score, and consequence.
- Educational progression is explicit, decodable, recoverable after error, and honest about mastery.
- Accessible and low-power modes preserve the same learning evidence and reward outcomes.
- Automated release gates exercise the same rich renderer and real input paths used by children.

## Pass 98 Audit Correction

The project previously treated feature inventory, asset counts, named motifs, and source-code wiring assertions as near-release evidence. The independent browser and code re-audit rejected that scoring method. A system can be sophisticated and still produce a repetitive or visually inconsistent game.

From Pass 98 onward:

- Generated routes must be labelled generated. A unique motif name is not authored level design.
- A visual score requires inspected desktop and phone evidence from the actual renderer.
- A gameplay score requires real movement and interaction, not DOM shortcuts alone.
- Regex tests may protect wiring, but they cannot prove quality, delight, legibility, animation craft, or level authorship.
- Child, teacher, assistive-technology, classroom-audio, and physical-device criteria cannot receive 10 without those participants or devices.
- Every score increase must name the visible defect fixed, the behavioural evidence added, and the remaining limitation.

## 30 July 2026 Book-World Hub Decision

The child-facing journey uses three original illustrated worlds belonging to the guided-reading universe:

- **Meadow Pals Farm:** stops 1-10, two five-stop chapters.
- **Dino Land: Sunny Hollow:** stops 11-20, two five-stop chapters.
- **Moonwood Tales:** stops 21-40, four five-stop chapters.

The map art is a hub layout, not a background for a single question. Every named junction launches a complete playable phonics stop and autosaves when the junction resolves. Replay can route the same weak grapheme through a different available mechanic instead of replaying an identical quiz. The forward journey remains open to the next curriculum junction; optional replay increases rewards and practice without trapping a lower-ESL child.

Every junction exposes at least four suitable mechanics, with a broad world mix drawn from sound hunting, movement routes, sorting, herding, delivery, construction, sequencing, memory, blending, spelling and story use. A chapter must still contain five genuinely different main verb grammars. Cosmetic variety does not count as mechanic variety.

The avatar is chosen independently of world. The initial choices are flagship book characters from Meadow Pals, Dino Pals and Moonwood Tales, and the child may change colour, expression, pose, pattern, clothing and accessories. Avatar choice is always free; unlockable customisation remains part of the reward economy. Residents belong to the book world in which they appear. No unrelated monster cast may be introduced as a shortcut.

Movement is direct four-way movement on keyboard and touch. Left/right and up/down use the same held-input system; route snapping must not cancel vertical travel. The active learning encounter may still set a forward boundary so a child cannot accidentally bypass the junction.

The replay and pacing model follows the useful principles found in Teach Your Monster to Read—short varied games, adaptive return of weak graphemes, rewards, and frequent saved progress—without copying its characters, maps, level scripts or visual assets. The product remains an original Literacy Guide game using its own books, curriculum and mechanics.

## 16 July 2026 Pixel-Direction Decision

The imperative Three.js implementation remains supported while a replacement direction is proved. New environment expansion must not continue inside the monolithic 3D scene by default. The preferred candidate is now a **top-down authored pixel world** inspired by the craft principles visible in Drakantos: fixed readable framing, dense hand-composed maps, strong silhouettes, layered occlusion, Y-sorting, extensive character animation, environmental storytelling, disciplined palettes, and restrained effects.

This is not permission to copy Drakantos characters, maps, lore, interface, or combat. Sound Seekers retains its brighter child-safe tone, Beastie identity, phonics mechanics, progression, rewards, reporting, and accessibility contract.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. No release claim is based only on data existing or a unit test returning green.
2. No child-facing task may depend on text alone. Audio, staging, iconography, and action must carry the instruction.
3. A missed tap must never become a different action.
4. A child is never credited for a target they did not attempt.
5. No known-missing phonics audio may enter an audio-dependent task.
6. No renderer tier may make an answer harder to perceive than another tier.
7. Accessibility fallback must preserve learning intent, correction, mastery credit, rewards, and pacing.
8. One active journey uses one WebGL renderer and one scene lifecycle.
9. Tests may become stricter. They may not be weakened to accommodate implementation.
10. A workstream is complete only after its proof has been opened and inspected.

## Workstream 1: Interaction And Perceptual Integrity

### Problems to eliminate

- Rich-mode letters can wash into pale plinths, road, haze, and bloom.
- Target rim lights and post-processing can reduce rather than improve legibility.
- A raycast miss during an encounter falls through to ground movement.
- Collision can then submit an unintended object.
- Visual tests currently force low quality and bypass real pointer input.

### Required work

- Give every answer token a stable contrast contract: dark or light glyph chosen from measured luminance, restrained emissive light, visible silhouette, and non-bloom-dependent edge separation.
- Tune rich lighting, target lights, fog, exposure, and bloom against actual answer scenes, not travel beauty shots.
- Add forgiving invisible hit proxies around answer objects without enlarging their visible geometry.
- During an active encounter, consume every pointer event. A miss does nothing except optional gentle recenter feedback; it never becomes movement.
- Keep collision selection for deliberate locomotion, but require the collision system to be armed, stage-correct, and unambiguous.
- Add real pointer-driven desktop and phone tests in rich mode.
- Add pixel-luminance assertions for glyph/background separation and canvas non-blankness.

### Acceptance result

- Every choice in every Seedwake stage is fully readable at rich, balanced, low, and 2D tiers.
- A tap 12-18 CSS pixels around the visible token selects that token.
- A tap outside all hit proxies leaves the Beastie stationary and records no attempt.
- The same pointer test passes at 390x844 and 1194x834.
- No rich-mode capture contains clipped, overexposed, or unreadable graphemes.

### Required proof

- Rich and balanced screenshots for every Seedwake encounter at phone and tablet sizes.
- Pointer miss, near-hit, and exact-hit integration tests.
- Contrast report with measured glyph and immediate-background luminance.

## Workstream 2: Learning Load And Task Pacing

### Problems to eliminate

- A stop introduces four new sounds before the child acts.
- Chapter one requires 62 correct selections plus guide advances.
- Multi-stage expansion defeats the intended response budget.
- Rook Stones can become a 20-plus-action endurance task.
- Guide presentation is a reading-heavy panel rather than a brief character cue.

### Required work

- Treat each stop as a short play session with a hard child-action budget.
- Seedwake target: 6-10 assessed actions per stop, 35-45 for the complete chapter.
- Introduce sounds just in time: one short guide cue immediately before the first meaningful use, then retrieval in a different context.
- Remove the four-screen pre-lesson. Keep optional replay and optional letter-name access outside the primary flow.
- Cap word-building to one high-value word sequence per encounter unless remediation is triggered.
- Ensure a sound receives enough evidence across later review rather than exhausting evidence in its introduction stop.
- Preserve the two-day mastery rule. Passing a stop must not imply mastery.
- Add generated action-count budgets to release checks.

### Acceptance result

- No Seedwake stop exceeds 10 required correct actions on a clean run.
- No single encounter exceeds 5 required correct actions.
- The first meaningful child action occurs within 30 seconds of entering the trail.
- Every instruction is eight words or fewer and understandable through audio plus staging.
- A clean chapter playthrough is approximately 12-18 minutes, not an hour-long repetition loop.

### Required proof

- Machine-generated action-budget report for s1-s40.
- Timed clean-play report for s1-s5.
- Video or frame sequence showing just-in-time teaching followed by immediate action.

## Workstream 3: Genuinely Different Physical Verbs

### Problems to eliminate

- Find, jump, build, and conduct mostly resolve as walking into a stationary choice.
- Different handler names do not guarantee different player skill or control feel.
- The resident performance changes, but the child action often does not.

### Required work

- Define a control and evidence contract for every production verb.
- Seedwake must include five distinct interaction grammars:
  - Hunt: explore a bounded patch, listen, reveal, then enter.
  - Jump: time or direct a committed leap between readable blooms.
  - Deliver: pick up, visibly carry, navigate, and deposit.
  - Build: select and place ordered pieces into persistent world slots.
  - Conduct: repeat an audible and visible rhythm with timed input windows.
- Use shared curriculum content without sharing the same completion path.
- Provide keyboard, touch, and switch-compatible equivalents.
- Give each action a unique animation, sound family, camera behaviour, and success consequence.

### Acceptance result

- No two Seedwake verbs share the same primary input state machine.
- A player can identify the current verb from a muted five-second clip.
- Each verb produces a persistent visible change in the world.
- Failure feedback teaches the required action without punishment or arbitrary reset.

### Required proof

- State-machine tests demonstrating observably different rules.
- Five short action clips, viewed side by side.
- Input-parity tests for keyboard and pointer/touch.

## Workstream 4: Journey Continuity And Runtime Stability

### Problems to eliminate

- Gate handoff can mount two complete WebGL worlds simultaneously.
- Two renderers, PMREM environments, composers, models, and shadow maps can coexist.
- Encounter timeouts can outlive scene teardown.
- Static hardware heuristics do not detect long frames or context pressure.

### Required work

- Replace the journey-layer renderer stack with one persistent canvas, renderer, composer, camera, and asset manager.
- Transition chapters by swapping disposable scene content under the persistent runtime.
- Preload data and asset promises, not a second live renderer.
- Centralise all gameplay timers in a disposable scheduler or abort scope.
- Cancel delayed correction, stage advance, word audio, and next-beat callbacks on stop change and unmount.
- Handle `webglcontextlost` and `webglcontextrestored` with a calm fallback.
- Add frame-time sampling and automatic downward tier adaptation.
- Keep gate progression completion-driven and checkpoint-safe.

### Acceptance result

- Exactly one WebGL context exists throughout a five-stop journey.
- Crossing every Seedwake gate produces no menu reset, blank frame, spin, or duplicate input.
- No callback from the previous stop mutates the next stop.
- A 20-minute automated soak completes without increasing live renderer, geometry, material, or listener counts.
- Slow-device simulation automatically lowers quality before input responsiveness degrades.

### Required proof

- Renderer/context count assertion during full journey.
- Gate-crossing integration test using real input.
- Timer-disposal tests.
- Heap/resource snapshots before and after a repeated five-stop soak.

## Workstream 7: Accessibility And Low-Power Parity

### Problems to eliminate

- Accessible 2D uses different legacy mini-games from the 3D physical tasks.
- High-contrast state exists but has no complete UI or rendering effect.
- Accessible mode selection is buried and child-facing.
- A modal can leave background controls focusable.
- Rich canvas choices have no equivalent semantic object representation.

### Required work

- Build one mechanic model with 3D and 2D presentations, not two separate games.
- Ensure both presentations emit the same target, shell/evidence id, correction state, progress, reward, and telemetry.
- Add a semantic DOM choice layer for every active 3D task, visually hidden when appropriate but keyboard and assistive-technology operable.
- Implement high contrast, reduced motion, captions/text reinforcement, focus visibility, and scalable UI.
- Move accessibility controls to an always-reachable settings route and remember them per child/device as appropriate.
- Add focus trapping and background inertness to dialogs.
- Detect WebGL failure and context loss without losing progress.

### Acceptance result

- The same scripted answers produce identical mastery and rewards in 3D and 2D.
- Every task is completable without precision pointing, colour, motion, or sound alone.
- Keyboard focus never enters content behind a modal.
- High-contrast mode passes WCAG contrast checks for all essential UI and answer tokens.

### Required proof

- Cross-presentation parity tests.

## Engineering Structure Required To Support The Work

`QuestHub.jsx` must not absorb the pixel conversion. The pixel path establishes the replacement ownership boundary in `QuestPixelWorld.jsx`, `questPixelRuntime.js`, and `questPixelAvatar.js`; future authored-map and mechanic expansion belongs in similarly focused modules. The target ownership boundaries remain:

- `QuestRuntime`: persistent renderer, composer, camera, frame loop, resize, context handling, and disposal.
- `QuestScene`: one disposable stop/chapter scene graph.
- `QuestInput`: pointer, keyboard, semantic choice, hit proxies, and control-mode state.
- `QuestLocomotion`: route-relative movement, acceleration, collision, and animation intent.
- `QuestEncounterDirector`: task state, correction, staging, timers, and completion.
- `QuestCameraDirector`: travel, teaching, encounter, gate, and ceremony shots.
- `QuestCharacterDirector`: player appearance, resident rigs, animation clips, reactions, and gear.
- `QuestHudModel`: small serialisable projection of the immediate child-facing state.
- `QuestAssetManager`: cached source assets, clones, LODs, progress, abort, and disposal.

Legacy `TrailWalk`, `WorldScene`, `Prop`, and `Props` must either be removed after proving no active imports or explicitly owned as a supported fallback. Dead parallel implementations are not documentation.

The current 3D renderer is a legacy-supported presentation during the pixel evaluation. It may receive critical fixes, but new chapter art production should target the pixel runtime unless a measured review overturns this decision.

## Loop Protocol

For each workstream:

1. Reproduce the current failure and save the proof.
2. Write or strengthen the failing automated gate.
3. Implement the smallest complete system change that fixes the child-facing behaviour.
4. Run focused tests.
5. Open and inspect desktop and phone visual proof.
6. Play the affected flow manually with correct and wrong actions.
7. Run global tests, lint, build, quest integrity, asset, music, and release gates.
8. Record DONE, PARTIAL, or BLOCKED in the release report with evidence.

## Definition Of Release Ready

Sound Seekers is release ready only when:

- Every critical and major review finding in this document is DONE.
- Every enabled chapter meets the same production contract.
- All automated gates are green without skipped or weakened assertions.
- All required proof has been opened and inspected.

## Desired Final Result

The finished Sound Seekers experience should feel like one coherent game: the Beastie the child created moves with weight through authored places; residents stage short memorable physical problems; phonics instructions arrive through sound and action; every tap does exactly what the child intended; correct reading changes the world; rewards build identity and useful capability; and the adult evidence remains rigorous without becoming visible assessment machinery.
