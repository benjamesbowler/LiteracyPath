# LiteracyPath terms of service

> **Status:** EXTERNAL-READY DRAFT — QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED
> **Version:** 2026-07-24
> **Effective date:** not set; this draft is not in force

These draft terms are written for school-managed use. Contracting identity,
address, governing law, commercial terms, and notices must be completed from
`LEGAL_DEPLOYMENT_FACTS.md` and approved by counsel before publication.

## 1. Parties and acceptance

The service provider is the verified contracting operator recorded in the final
order form (“LiteracyPath”, “we”, or “us”). The customer is the school, district,
education authority, organisation, or authorised individual accepting an order
form (“Customer”). These terms, the order form, privacy notice, and any executed
Data Processing Addendum form the agreement.

The person accepting the agreement represents that they have authority to bind
the Customer and authorise school-managed learner use. A child cannot accept
these terms or open a teacher account.

## 2. Service and authorised users

LiteracyPath provides early-literacy activities, classroom administration,
learning evidence, progress views, and reports. Customer may permit authorised
staff and learners to use the service for Customer’s educational purposes.
Customer controls which learners are enrolled, which staff can access their
records, and the educational decisions made from reports.

The service supports teachers; it does not diagnose a disability, replace a
qualified educator or clinician, guarantee a learning outcome, or make a
high-stakes placement decision without human review.

## 3. School and operator responsibilities

LiteracyPath will:

- process Customer Data only to provide, secure, support, and lawfully operate
  the service, subject to the agreement and DPA;
- not sell Customer Data or use learner data for advertising, marketing
  profiles, or an unrelated commercial purpose;
- maintain the controls described in `SECURITY_SUMMARY.md`;
- provide reasonable assistance with access, correction, export, restriction,
  and deletion requests; and
- notify Customer of material service, privacy, or subprocessor changes through
  the agreed notice route.

Customer will:

- use an institutionally approved procurement and consent process;
- give required notices and select a valid local legal basis;
- provide only data necessary for the educational purpose;
- keep staff credentials confidential and promptly remove access that is no
  longer required;
- use picture-code learner credentials only in the supervised, low-risk context
  for which they are designed;
- verify the accuracy and educational meaning of exported reports; and
- contact LiteracyPath promptly about suspected misuse, security events, or
  rights requests.

Nothing in the agreement transfers LiteracyPath’s own legal obligations to a
school. Each party remains responsible for the duties that apply to its role.

## 4. Child privacy and acceptable use

Customer may use the service with children only through the approved
school-managed workflow. Customer and users must not:

- create a learner account outside an authorised school or adult-managed context;
- enter unnecessary sensitive data, free-form safeguarding notes, medical
  diagnoses, home contact details, precise location, or learner photographs;
- attempt to identify pseudonymous leaderboard entries;
- share class codes or picture credentials outside the intended class;
- probe, disrupt, reverse engineer, scrape, or bypass access controls;
- upload unlawful, harmful, infringing, discriminatory, or malicious material;
- use reports as the sole basis for a high-stakes decision; or
- use the service for advertising, surveillance, or unrelated profiling.

LiteracyPath may suspend access reasonably believed to threaten children,
security, law, or other customers, while preserving notice and remediation where
appropriate.

## 5. Customer Data, privacy, and data rights

Customer retains its rights in Customer Data. Customer instructs LiteracyPath to
process that data for the service functions selected by Customer. The DPA
governs personal data. The privacy notice explains current categories, purposes,
providers, and choices.

If a parent, guardian, learner, or regulator contacts LiteracyPath about school
records, LiteracyPath may verify and route the request through Customer unless
law requires a direct response. Customer must not use the service to avoid an
individual’s applicable rights.

## 6. Service data and product improvement

LiteracyPath may use privacy-minimal operational information to secure and
maintain the service, such as redacted error fingerprints, release versions, and
aggregate health counts. LiteracyPath will not use identifiable learner data to
train an AI model, create advertising profiles, or develop an unrelated
commercial product without a new lawful basis, notice, contractual authority,
and any required consent.

Any proposed use of de-identified or aggregated information must be documented,
reasonably protected against re-identification, and approved in the final
agreement. This draft does not itself authorise an additional use.

## 7. Intellectual property and feedback

LiteracyPath and its licensors retain rights in the service, curriculum,
software, media, and documentation. Customer receives a limited,
non-exclusive, non-transferable right to use the service during the agreement.
Customer is responsible for having rights to material it supplies.

Customer may provide feedback. Final terms must specify whether and how
LiteracyPath may use that feedback without exposing Customer Data or identifying
a child, school, staff member, or family.

## 8. Third-party providers

Approved providers are listed in `SUBPROCESSORS.md`. Their services may be
subject to their own technical availability. LiteracyPath remains responsible
for its contractual duties and will follow the DPA’s subprocessor process.
Authoring-only AI and media tools are not authorised to receive Customer Data.

## 9. Security and incidents

Both parties will use reasonable safeguards appropriate to their roles.
Customer acknowledges that no online service is risk-free. LiteracyPath will
follow `INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md` and the executed DPA for incidents involving
Customer Data. Security testing must be authorised in writing.

## 10. Availability, support, and changes

The final order form must state support channels, service levels, maintenance,
fees, taxes, renewal, and refund terms. LiteracyPath may improve the service but
will not materially reduce contracted privacy or security protections during a
paid term without the process agreed with Customer.

Beta, preview, demo, and offline-fallback features must be identified as such and
must not be relied on for mandatory records unless the order form expressly
allows it.

## 11. Term, suspension, export, and deletion

The final agreement must define its term and termination rights. On expiry or
termination, Customer will have the agreed period to export Customer Data.
LiteracyPath will then return or delete personal data under the DPA, except where
law requires retention. Backup expiry and deletion propagation must follow the
verified deployment schedule; they must not be promised until those facts are
confirmed.

## 12. Confidentiality

Each party will protect the other party’s confidential information with
reasonable care, use it only for the agreement, and disclose it only to people
who need it and are bound to protect it. Legal disclosure is permitted after
notice where law allows. Customer Data and non-public security information are
confidential.

## 13. Warranties and disclaimers

Each party warrants that it has authority to enter the agreement. LiteracyPath
warrants that it will provide the service with reasonable skill and care and
materially follow its documentation.

Counsel must adapt all mandatory consumer, education, and public-authority
rights. Any disclaimer must be lawful, conspicuous, and must not disclaim data
protection, confidentiality, child-safety, or other obligations that cannot
lawfully be excluded.

## 14. Liability, indemnity, and insurance

The final agreement must contain counsel-approved liability exclusions, caps,
carve-outs, indemnities, and insurance requirements appropriate to children’s
data and the target jurisdiction. No cap or exclusion is proposed by this draft,
and none should be inferred.

## 15. Governing law and disputes

The final agreement must identify governing law, venue, escalation, and any
public-sector requirements from verified deployment facts. It must preserve
non-waivable rights and regulator authority.

## 16. Notices, assignment, and general terms

The final agreement must specify valid notice addresses and methods. Neither
party may assign the agreement except as finally agreed. The final boilerplate
should address force majeure, waiver, severability, order of precedence,
amendment, counterparts, and entire agreement without overriding the DPA.
