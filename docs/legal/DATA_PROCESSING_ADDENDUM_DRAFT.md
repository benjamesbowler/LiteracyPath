# LiteracyPath data processing addendum

> **Status:** EXTERNAL-READY DRAFT — QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED
> **Version:** 2026-07-24
> **Execution status:** unsigned and not legally binding

This Data Processing Addendum (“DPA”) is intended to attach to the final
LiteracyPath agreement. Defined terms follow applicable data protection law and
the agreement. Counsel must adapt the clauses, annexes, transfers, and signatures
for the Customer and jurisdiction.

## 1. Scope and order of precedence

This DPA applies when LiteracyPath processes personal data on Customer’s behalf
to provide the service. If this DPA conflicts with the service terms on personal
data protection, this DPA controls. It does not decide a party’s role where the
law determines the role from actual conduct.

## 2. Roles

For school-selected learner and staff data, the expected model is:

- Customer is controller, educational agency/institution, or equivalent body;
- LiteracyPath is processor/service provider acting on documented instructions;
- approved infrastructure providers are subprocessors; and
- each party is independently responsible for processing for which it determines
  its own purposes and essential means.

If LiteracyPath uses personal data beyond Customer’s instructions for its own
purpose, it may be a controller for that processing regardless of the contract
label. Any such processing requires a documented role, lawful basis, notice, and
review before launch.

## 3. Documented instructions

Customer instructs LiteracyPath to collect, host, organise, retrieve, display,
adapt for learning, secure, support, export, and delete the data described in
Annex 1 solely to provide the contracted service. Configuration choices, support
requests, and written directions that are consistent with the agreement are
additional instructions.

LiteracyPath will notify Customer if it reasonably believes an instruction
violates applicable law and may pause that instruction while the parties resolve
it. LiteracyPath will process outside instructions only when legally required
and, where permitted, will notify Customer before doing so.

## 4. Purpose limitation and prohibited uses

LiteracyPath will not:

- sell or rent Customer personal data;
- use learner data for targeted advertising or marketing profiles;
- use Customer personal data for an unrelated commercial purpose;
- use identifiable learner data to train an AI model;
- attempt to re-identify data that the parties validly treat as de-identified; or
- combine Customer personal data with unrelated third-party data except on
  documented instructions and with a valid legal basis.

## 5. Confidentiality and personnel

LiteracyPath will limit access to people who need it for the service, ensure they
are bound by confidentiality, provide role-appropriate privacy and security
guidance, and remove access promptly when no longer required.

## 6. Security

LiteracyPath will maintain measures appropriate to risk, including the controls
in Annex 2 and `SECURITY_SUMMARY.md`. It will regularly test relevant controls,
address material findings based on risk, and not materially reduce the overall
protection of Customer personal data during the term.

No summary or automated test is a security certification. Customer may request
current evidence through the agreed review process.

## 7. Subprocessors

Customer grants general written authorisation for the subprocessors in
`SUBPROCESSORS.md`, subject to completion of verified provider and transfer
facts. LiteracyPath will:

- contractually require materially equivalent data-protection obligations;
- remain responsible for each subprocessor’s performance of those obligations;
- provide advance notice through the agreed channel before adding or replacing
  a subprocessor that processes Customer personal data; and
- allow a reasonable, good-faith objection based on data-protection grounds,
  followed by mitigation, an alternative where reasonably available, or
  termination of the affected service as finally agreed.

The final notice period and remedy must be set in the executed DPA.

## 8. Individual rights and school records

Taking account of the nature of processing, LiteracyPath will reasonably assist
Customer with verified access, correction, export, restriction, objection, and
deletion requests. LiteracyPath will not disclose a learner’s record to an
unverified requester. It may route a request to Customer when Customer controls
the education record, unless applicable law requires LiteracyPath to respond.

The executed DPA must state response channels, verification, service levels,
secure delivery, exception handling, and completion evidence.

## 9. Government and third-party requests

Unless prohibited by law, LiteracyPath will promptly notify Customer of a
legally binding request for Customer personal data, review the request for
validity, disclose only what is legally required, and reasonably support
Customer’s protective efforts. The final clause must reflect provider-chain
limitations and applicable law.

## 10. Personal data incidents

LiteracyPath will follow `INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md`. After confirming a personal data
incident affecting Customer Data, LiteracyPath will notify Customer without
undue delay. The proposed operational target is initial notice within 24 hours
of confirmation, but counsel and the incident owner must approve the contractual
commitment.

Notice will include available information about nature, timing, affected data
and people, likely consequences, containment, contact, and recommended actions.
Information may be supplied in phases. LiteracyPath will preserve evidence,
cooperate with Customer’s assessment and notices, and provide a post-incident
record appropriate to risk.

## 11. Return, deletion, and retention

During the term, Customer may use supported export and deletion workflows. On
termination or instruction, LiteracyPath will return or delete personal data
unless law requires retention. It will propagate deletion to active systems and
subprocessors and allow protected backups to expire under the verified schedule.
Restored backups must replay deletion/tombstone controls before service.

The executed DPA must attach confirmed active-data, inactivity, end-of-year,
termination, log, and backup periods. Indefinite retention is not authorised.

## 12. Assistance, DPIAs, and consultation

LiteracyPath will provide reasonable information to help Customer with security,
breach assessment, DPIAs, children’s best-interests assessments, regulator
consultation, and records of processing, taking account of the service and the
information available to LiteracyPath.

## 13. Demonstrating compliance and audits

LiteracyPath will make current, relevant evidence available under reasonable
confidentiality protections. The final DPA should prefer recent independent
reports and written responses, then allow a proportionate audit if those are
insufficient. Audits must protect other customers, security, and confidentiality,
avoid unreasonable disruption, and allocate cost as counsel agrees. A material
confirmed failure requires a remediation plan.

## 14. International transfers

No restricted transfer is approved merely by signing this draft. Before
processing, the parties must document provider locations and remote access,
identify restricted transfers, and select a valid mechanism. Depending on the
deployment this may include adequacy, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, the
UK International Data Transfer Agreement or UK Addendum, and a transfer risk
assessment with supplementary measures.

The completed transfer annex and provider mechanisms must be attached to the
executed DPA. If the chosen mechanism is invalidated, the parties will cooperate
on a lawful replacement and suspend affected transfers where required.

## 15. FERPA, COPPA, and education-specific terms

For applicable US school deployments, LiteracyPath will process education
records only for the school-authorised educational purpose, under Customer’s
direct control as required by the selected FERPA exception, and will not
redisclose them except as authorised or required by law. LiteracyPath will
support review and deletion and will not shift its COPPA duties to Customer.

The school must independently determine the applicable FERPA exception, annual
notice fit, PPRA obligations, state-law addenda, and authority to provide school
consent under COPPA. The operator must provide the required direct notice and
meet its own COPPA obligations.

## Annex 1 — processing details

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Subject matter | School-managed early-literacy service |
| Duration | Contract term plus approved export, deletion, legal-hold, and backup-expiry periods |
| Nature | Collection, storage, organisation, retrieval, adaptation for learning, display, reporting, export, security monitoring, support, and deletion |
| Purposes | Authentication, class administration, learning delivery, recommendations, progress/evidence, reporting, access security, support, and reliability |
| Data subjects | Learners, teachers, school administrators, authorised support contacts, and parents/guardians making requests |
| Teacher/admin data | Email, display name, school, role/approval, class configuration, settings, account and service activity |
| Learner data | First/display name, class, credential material, accessibility settings, progress, answers, mastery, engagement, assessment evidence, reports, interventions, and game/reward state |
| Operational data | Privacy-minimal access events, redacted error fingerprints, release identity, timestamps, and sync health |
| Special/sensitive data | Not intentionally requested; accessibility settings may reveal support needs and must be treated with additional care |
| Frequency | As users access the service and as configured jobs maintain it |
| Deletion | Customer instruction, configured retention, termination, verified request, and backup expiry |

## Annex 2 — minimum technical and organisational measures

- tenant ownership and row-level access controls;
- teacher authentication, approval state, and least-privilege admin functions;
- scoped learner sessions, expiring class access, throttling, and generic denial;
- transport encryption through configured providers;
- content security and browser security headers;
- data minimisation, pseudonymous leaderboard identities, and no ad trackers;
- privacy-minimal logs and redacted operational error payloads;
- immutable/versioned formal assessment evidence;
- tested code changes, migration reconstruction, deterministic fixtures, and
  release gates;
- recovery objectives, isolated restore procedure, and deletion replay;
- incident triage, evidence preservation, notification, and lessons learned;
- controlled provider inventory and change review; and
- secure deletion and retention controls once the A8.9 operational schedule is
  implemented and verified.

## Annex 3 — parties, contacts, and transfers

The executed DPA must identify both legal entities, addresses, roles, privacy and
security contacts, signatures, effective date, provider regions, transfer
destinations, transfer mechanisms, and completed supplementary-measures review.
Those facts remain in `LEGAL_DEPLOYMENT_FACTS.md` until verified.
