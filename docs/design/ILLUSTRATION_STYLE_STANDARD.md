# Illustration style standard

**Authority:** current product-owner art direction, 29 October 2026.

New and replacement app illustrations use **bold, clean, clear 2D cartoon art**.
This applies to Guided Reading, Story Quests, assessment pictures, vocabulary,
phonics, Hollow, maps, illustrated game scenery, props and teaching images.

Use crisp continuous outlines, clear silhouettes and flat clean colors. Where
depth is needed, use a small number of smooth cel-shaded shapes. Keep the focal
action obvious at the actual child-facing size. A clean cartoon character on a
painted background does not satisfy this rule: the whole image must follow it.

Do not request or accept painterly rendering, gouache, watercolor, brush daubs,
patchy paint, visible brushwork, paper/canvas grain, embossed or impasto relief,
pebbled surfaces, fuzzy edges or hyper-textured material rendering. Show wood,
fur, stone and foliage with simple outlined shapes and restrained flat marks.
Necessary learning contrasts such as rough/smooth must remain explicit without
introducing simulated paint or embossed surfaces.

Keep exact scene meaning, character identity, anatomy, scale, lighting, safe
framing, transparency, registration and seamless tiling requirements. Clear
cartoon style does not authorize simplified-away teaching evidence or generic
replacement scenes. Photographic evidence and existing modeled 3D game assets
retain their separate contracts; this instruction corrects illustration style.

The modeled 3D exception requires model or rendering provenance. An illustrated
sprite sheet or backdrop remains an illustration when displayed on a billboard
inside a 3D renderer. Descriptions such as "physical", "felt" or "toy-console"
do not establish a modeled source or exempt simulated paint and embossed detail.
A sprite atlas rendered from an actual model keeps that model's contract.

The owner's 30 October 2026 direction requires neutral subject matter and
settings. Ordinary variation in skin tone, hair and family appearance is
welcome. Do not use racial or ethnic caricatures, identity-themed costumes,
religious clothing, religious symbols or buildings (including mosques and
churches), or national, religious or political flags. Plain game-course
pennants and checkered racing flags are permitted. Use everyday clothing,
ordinary homes, schools, community spaces and unbranded objects. Different
foods and names do not require cultural or religious ceremony. This direction
supersedes earlier collection-specific religious or festival instructions.

This standard supersedes older requests for painterly art, tactile material
detail, soft outlines, paper/gouache texture, "not flat vector" or "never flat
cel art" in illustration briefs. Historical prompts and generation receipts
remain provenance only. They must not be reused as current art direction.
The sealed Sound Seekers V2 prompt snapshots are retained to validate earlier
receipts, not as instructions for a new generation batch.

The reusable executable prompt wording is
[`CLEAN_CARTOON_ILLUSTRATION_STYLE`](../../src/content/illustrationStyle.js).
Reusable JSON job prompts follow the same rule. Where a source record retains
its original generation prompt, `sourcePrompt` records that history and `prompt`
is the corrected instruction for future use. Updating a prompt does not certify
or regenerate the existing pixels.

Before accepting replacement art, inspect both the subject and the background
at full size and at its app display size. Reject the prohibited finish even if
the file decodes and the prompt used the correct words.

## Physical logic and teaching meaning

Review the whole depicted situation, not only the focal object's style. People
must be doing an ordinary, coherent action in a suitable setting: a child eating
breakfast belongs at a meal table, not at a lesson desk surrounded by classmates
doing written work. Do not invent unrelated background activity to fill space.

Account for every carried object and contact. One child using one spoon must not
also have a second unexplained spoon in the bowl. Reject duplicate utensils,
floating/fused props, impossible grips, extra limbs, broken object geometry,
unexplained count changes and pseudo-writing. Check fingers and hands at native
size when a hand performs the teaching action.

For a word or assessment cue, show the actual represented object or action: a
crown alone is not a king, a stethoscope alone is not a vet, and a cat cannot stand
for cut. Preserve exact counts, spatial relationships, target meaning and any
necessary scientific or safety evidence. A visually attractive but incorrect
cue must be replaced. A valid fantasy or game mechanic needs its established
story/game context; that does not excuse accidental impossible details.

Record the exact reviewed bytes and intended meaning. Automated file/hash checks
support this review; they do not prove semantic correctness or a subjective
percentage of image quality.
