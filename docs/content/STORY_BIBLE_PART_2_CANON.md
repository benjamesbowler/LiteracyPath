# Story Bible — Part II: Literacy Guide Worlds and Character Canon

**Status:** Canonical continuity and production standard
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Applies to:** Every story, Story Quest, game, image, animation, narration and marketing
asset using Literacy Guide characters or worlds

This is the product-specific canon. It defines who the characters are, how they look and
behave, which world they belong to, their relative scale, and the visual/audio continuity
rules that prevent drift. A release that breaks a **MUST** rule does not ship.

Part I contains the separate research-led children's-story writing standard.

## 1. Canon hierarchy: never guess a character

When sources disagree, use this order:

1. Approved character model sheet with a version number.
2. Approved book-world visual bible and scale chart.
3. Approved, published page sequence for that character.
4. Current Story Quest cover and page art.
5. Current story text and metadata.
6. Drafts, prompts and retired art.

If steps 1–4 conflict, STOP. Create a continuity issue and resolve the canon before writing
or generating more media. Do not average two designs together. Do not quietly choose the
more attractive one.

Every asset prompt MUST name:

- canonical character ID;
- species;
- relative height;
- fixed palette;
- fixed markings;
- fixed clothing and carried props;
- world;
- time of day;
- current story-state objects;
- allowed pose or expression;
- prohibited drift.

## 2. The three worlds

### 2.1 Sunny Meadow Farm — Level A

**Audience:** usually ages 4–6; low-level or early English readers
**Story character:** warm, concrete, farm-scale, playful and immediately readable
**Locations:** red barn, duck pond, mud wallow, big oak, hill, stone wall, hay, flower
meadow, little stream
**Visual language:** bright natural daylight, clean silhouettes, smooth classic
children's-cartoon finish, simple depth, uncluttered focal action; never embossed,
grainy, pebbled, relief-like, fake-canvas or over-sharpened
**Permitted magic:** character traits such as spring legs; no new unexplained magic system
**Typical goals:** find a hat, follow a map, reach a tree, help a friend, recover one object

Never put Moonwood magic, Dino Pals, volcanoes or Moonwood architecture into a Meadow
story unless a separately approved crossover explicitly explains it.

### 2.2 Sunny Hollow / Dino Land — Level B

**Audience:** usually ages 5–7; low-to-mid readers
**Story character:** physical comedy, clear social friction, stronger consequences, simple
dialogue
**Locations:** Cozy Cave, Berry Bush Corner, Big Flat Rock, Muddy Puddle Pool, stream,
fernwood and long meadow
**Visual language:** colourful prehistoric valley, rounded forms, smooth painted shapes,
cheerful colour, gentle 2D shading and large readable gestures; never embossed, grainy,
pebbled, relief-like, fake-canvas, hyper-textured or over-sharpened
**Permitted magic:** established character anatomy such as Bouncy’s spring legs; otherwise
events must follow the world’s physical rules
**Typical goals:** find food, make shelter, repair a mess, help two friends with different
wants, complete a plan

### 2.3 Moonwood — Level C

**Audience:** usually ages 7–9; mid-to-high readers
**Story character:** mystery, wonder, stronger causal chains, contrasting personalities,
earned emotion
**Locations:** Hollow Oak, Crystal Stream, Fog Marsh, Deep Dark, old roots, glow cave,
Fern’s garden and star rooms
**Visual language:** moonlit woodland fantasy in a bright, clean, classic children's-cartoon
rendering style, with smooth painted shapes, coherent scale, controlled glow and rich but
readable backgrounds; never embossed, grainy, pebbled, relief-like, fake-canvas,
hyper-textured or over-sharpened
**Permitted magic:** only established character abilities, artefacts and rules. A magical
door or potion must use the same rule throughout its story.
**Typical goals:** follow a clue, restore or locate a natural light, repair a magical mistake,
reunite a creature, open a rule-bound door
**Narrative page profile:** one connected, child-clear paragraph per page, normally 24–34
words in 2–4 short sentences and intended to wrap to roughly 4–5 reader lines. Every page
must name the active character or object, explain the immediate cause or reaction, connect
to the preceding beat and prepare the next. Cryptic fragments, unexplained pronouns and
compressed report-like summaries are prohibited.
**Narrative tense and pronouns:** Moonwood book narration uses simple past consistently;
present tense remains natural inside dialogue or for a stable fact. Glimmer uses they/them
pronouns in every book.

“It is magic” is not a substitute for cause and effect.

visual source of truth wherever one exists.

### 3.6 Current canon conflicts that must be resolved

These are known issues, not permission to continue the drift:

1. **Two characters are called Bouncy.** Internally and in every asset manifest, use
   `MEADOW-BOUNCY` and `DINO-BOUNCY`. Never search or prompt with “Bouncy” alone.
2. **Dewdrop has incompatible body designs in existing Moonwood art.** No new Dewdrop media
   ships until one master model is approved.
3. **Moonwood rendering style varies between flat cel art and painterly storybook art.**
   Choose one master style and re-render sequences rather than mixing them.
4. **Clucky’s colour and relative size drift in existing Meadow art.** Lock one model and
   scale chart before the next Clucky sequence.
5. **Some spring-legged characters have been drawn without feet.** Every approved model
   must show how feet attach to coils in standing, jumping and landing poses.

## 4. Relative scale locks

Scale is part of identity. It cannot be improvised page by page.

### Meadow reference order

From smallest to largest, unless an approved model sheet specifies exact ratios:

`Tiny < Brave ≈ Splashy < Shy < Cuddly < Clucky < Muddy ≈ Speedy < lamb characters`

Spring extension changes height during a jump, not body size.

### Dino reference

- Grumpy is low and heavy, not tiny.
- Bouncy is a small young dinosaur.
- Chompy and Sunny are medium young dinosaurs.
- Dozy, Fancy and Wiggly use the approved group scale chart.
- A camera close-up cannot be treated as a change in body size.

### Moonwood reference

`small creature/animal < Pip/Burrow/Luna < Wren/Flint/Fern < Stone`

Stone should read as exceptionally large in every shared frame.

Every multi-character image prompt MUST state relative height in words or ratios.

## 5. Illustration bible

### 5.1 Every page image must

- use bright, clean, classic children's-cartoon rendering with smooth color, crisp
  silhouettes and controlled shading; reject embossed, grainy, pebbled, relief-like,
  fake-canvas, hyper-textured or over-sharpened finishes;
- depict the exact current story beat;
- show the protagonist and the state object when the text depends on them;
- preserve character model, colour, markings, clothing and scale;
- preserve location, weather and time unless the story changes them;
- show the consequence of the preceding choice;
- keep essential faces, limbs and props within an 8% safe area;
- use one coherent camera and one moment;
- remain readable on a small laptop and tablet;
- contain no malformed text, extra limbs, duplicate characters or fused bodies;
- keep inanimate scenery faceless;
- use clean, large visual evidence rather than tiny clues.

### 5.2 Image and text division of labour

Text tells what must be decoded. Art shows:

- spatial relationship;
- expression and body language;
- scale;
- object state;
- setting;
- consequence;
- secondary humour.

The art must not contradict the words. The words must not describe an action, character or
object absent from the image.

### 5.3 Sequence continuity sheet

Before generating page 1, create one locked sheet containing:

- front, side and three-quarter character views;
- neutral, happy, worried, angry, surprised and thinking expressions;
- standing, walking, running, reaching and holding poses;
- fixed palette values;
- relative scale lineup;
- recurring prop turnarounds;
- location palette and time-of-day key;
- “never” examples.

All page prompts reference that sheet. Regenerating a later page without the sheet is
prohibited.

### 5.4 Visible writing inside art

- Include visible words only when the plot requires them.
- Specify the exact wording, spelling, case and orientation.
- If wording has no plot role, use a purposeful drawing, diagram or non-letter texture.
- Never leave a map, book or sign blank when the story says the character reads it.
- Never accept pseudo-writing that looks like misspelled English.

## 6. Audio bible

Every story page or Story Quest scene MUST have:

- one visible speaker/replay control;
- narration that matches the displayed text word for word;
- the approved production voice;
- stable pronunciation of every character and place name;
- consistent loudness;
- no clipped first or last phoneme;
- no stale recording after a text edit.

Production voice:

- Google Cloud Text-to-Speech;
- `en-US-Chirp3-HD-Leda`;
- warm, natural General American delivery;
- clear phrasing;
- expressive enough to carry meaning, never exaggerated character acting;
- approximately 145–155 words per minute, with slower phrasing for Level A.

Audio release checks:

1. Hash the exact visible text used to create the clip.
2. Store voice, engine, date and text hash in the manifest.
3. Fail the build when visible text changes without a matching new clip.
4. Check every referenced file exists and is non-empty.
5. Listen to all character names, homographs and sentence endings.
6. Never silently fall back to stale narration.

## 7. Automated release gates

The repository MUST fail checks for:

- missing or duplicate quest/page IDs;
- unreachable pages;
- missing targets;
- narrative loops other than explicit “Read again”;
- routes with no ending;
- endings without both “Read again” and “Finish”;
- two choices leading to the same scene;
- duplicate choice labels;
- path length outside the reading band;
- page/line/choice length outside the reading band;
- undeclared or unused target vocabulary;
- forbidden Level A grammar;
- missing image;
- missing narration;
- narration text-hash mismatch;
- duplicate character names without world-qualified canon IDs;
- choice branches that merge before showing a consequence;
- state contradictions at merge pages.

Automation can catch structural defects. It cannot decide whether a joke works, whether a
character feels alive or whether a page is beautiful. Those remain accountable
editorial evidence gates; named human sign-off is not required.
