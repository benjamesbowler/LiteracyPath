# Sound Seekers competitive benchmark

Status: Research input, not product authority

Date: 2026-09-01

Scope: Early-literacy learning games and transferable 2D adventure design

Governing design: [Sound Seekers proper educational game design](../specs/2026-09-01-sound-seekers-proper-educational-game-design.md)

## 1. What the benchmark changes

The strongest pattern is not a particular reward system or screen layout. The
most credible references make the learning action the consequential game
action, teach explicitly before expecting independence, keep directions and
feedback recoverable, and vary the context without changing what success
means. Polished adventure references add a second lesson: a child's action
should visibly change a coherent world.

The benchmark tightens the approved `SS-01` through `SS-20` requirements. It
does not create another design authority, validate a vendor's efficacy claims,
or authorize copied characters, art, dialogue, level layouts, or distinctive
mechanic expression.

## 3. Evidence boundary and caveats

- Official product pages establish public features and design intent, not
  comparative efficacy. Vendor case studies are not independent evidence.
- Teach Your Monster's rebuild posts describe work in progress. They are useful
  process evidence, not proof that the resulting experience works.
- The EEF GraphoGame trial is unusually useful because it separates engagement
  from literacy outcome: the absence of added impact means Sound Seekers must
  not use completion, enjoyment, or in-game accuracy as evidence of off-game
  transfer.
- The Duolingo ABC EDC study used a single-group pre/post design. Improvement is
  compatible with benefit but cannot be attributed to the app alone.
- Starfall's public report evaluates a broader curriculum in a cross-sectional
  comparison. It does not isolate the causal contribution of a game mechanic.
- Public information does not expose every paid lesson, adaptive rule, data
  policy, correction sequence, or accessibility behavior. This was a source
  benchmark, not a full hands-on audit of each commercial product.
- Entertainment games are references for action clarity, assistance, art,
  pacing, and world response only. They are not evidence for phonics pedagogy.

## 4. Research principles used

- The [National Reading Panel findings](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/publications/pubs/nrp/Pages/findings.aspx),
  [IES foundational reading guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc_foundationalreading_040717.pdf),
  and [EEF phonics synthesis](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/phonics)
  support explicit, systematic phonics connected to decoding, encoding,
  fluency, and text rather than incidental exposure.
- The UK Department for Education's [systematic synthetic phonics validation
  criteria](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/phonics-teaching-materials-core-criteria-and-self-assessment/validation-of-systematic-synthetic-phonics-programmes-supporting-documentation)
  warn that games should not be over-elaborate, too difficult, or so long that
  they pull attention away from reading and writing. This supports short,
  literacy-consequential loops rather than decorative complexity.
- Game-based phonics studies report promise but heterogeneous results and
  designs. The relevant conclusion is to instrument the exact learning action
  and test transfer separately, not to assume a game format is effective.
  Sources: [GraphoGame review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00132/full),
  [phonics game study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.720548/full).
- WCAG 2.2 requires a simple-pointer alternative to dragging and supports
  motion and color-independent access. Sources: [dragging movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html),
  [animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions),
  [use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html).
  W3C's [step-instruction pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o4p07-step-instructions/)
  is advisory rather than a conformance rule, but it reinforces keeping the
  current instruction available while the child performs it.

## 5. Implications for the approved requirements

| Requirement | Research implication |
| --- | --- |
| `SS-01` | Persistent repair should be the meaningful payoff. Ban coercive retention mechanics and expose a natural stopping point after each saved learning loop. |
| `SS-02` | Give each Sound Power a consequence-free, replayable first-use rehearsal that teaches its unique control and state model. |
| `SS-03` | Keep the target, sound boxes, placed units, rack, and replay control stable; provide select-then-place as well as drag. |
| `SS-04` | Preserve an independent first attempt, then make feedback specific and require a fresh action; never let animation or timing substitute for blending or segmenting. |
| `SS-05` | Test arrival, first use, Wonder moment, transfer, payoff, exit, and resume as separate formative slices with children before multiplying chapters. |
| `SS-06` | Vary meaningful context while holding the target invariant. Separate literacy difficulty from motor assistance, including keyboard, switch, touch, pointer, and simple-pointer placement. |
| `SS-07` | Author pronunciation and unit structure explicitly; commercial breadth is not a reason to accept spelling-derived guesses or ambiguous audio. |
| `SS-08` | Use a brief, low-stakes, reversible starting-path sample and explicit unscored teaching. It is guidance for practice, not placement or mastery. |
| `SS-09` | Record the literacy action and support at the moment it occurs. Keep enjoyment, motor events, and any off-game research outcome outside routine mastery. |
| `SS-10` | Count distinct learning domains, not mini-game names. Do not admit speed, collection, or reskinned selection as a new evidence path. |
| `SS-11` | Adapt from recent confusions and support history; do not adapt from coins, play time, reaction speed, or an unexplained global percentage. |
| `SS-12` | Errors should name the contrast, preserve earned state, lower irrelevant load, and invite another attempt without lives or narrative punishment. |
| `SS-13` | Keep the active instruction and a labelled replay control available until completion; visible, spoken, semantic, and recorded meanings must agree. |
| `SS-14` | Make heart-word coverage bounded and visible without pressuring the child to extend a session or treating deck completion as mastery. |
| `SS-15` | Move from phrase to connected text. Supported echo, shared, or repeated reading is participation/support evidence, not scored fluency or mastery. |
| `SS-16` | Let decoded meaning change props, residents, routes, and relationships; decoration alone does not establish comprehension. |
| `SS-17` | Schedule review from monotonic learning history and permit lossless exit after each loop; a daily streak must not control spacing or access. |
| `SS-18` | Use coherent biome identity and strong target hierarchy, but never color as the only state cue. Derive performance budgets from measured supported low-power devices. |
| `SS-19` | Use anticipation, contact, reaction, and a clear final state; reduced-motion replacements and color-independent icons/outlines must preserve meaning. |
| `SS-20` | Keep formative child sessions, listening, visual review, physical-device access, and low-power profiling as direct open evidence. Study off-game transfer only as a separately labelled research measure. |

## 6. Gaps the approved design needed to close

The approved design already defines truthful practice evidence, six genuine
learning systems, persistent world consequence, coherent 2D art, accessibility
parity, and separate direct release gates. The benchmark exposed narrower gaps:

1. Formative observed-child checkpoints were not named before the final direct
   gate, and missing direct evidence could be mistaken for implicit approval.
2. The fresh-journey starting path did not define a low-stakes, reversible
   calibration boundary.
3. A child's first encounter with each new Sound Power did not explicitly have
   a consequence-free control rehearsal.
4. Drag-and-drop parity, color-independent semantics, and persistent instruction
   replay were implied but not stated precisely enough to test.
5. The reward model did not explicitly ban streak loss, energy pressure,
   variable-ratio rewards, manipulative reminders, or forced continuation.
6. Natural stopping and lossless resume existed at the expedition level but
   needed an explicit child-visible endpoint after every learning loop.
7. Optional adult co-engagement was not distinguished from adult completion or
   child evidence.
8. Supported oral and repeated reading could be misread as scored fluency rather
   than participation with recorded support.
9. Low-power compatibility did not require measured device profiling or define
   which runtime signals must be observed.
10. Off-game transfer was named as a learning ambition but not isolated as a
    research measure that can never become routine game mastery.

These gaps are tightened in the governing specification's research addendum.
They remain open implementation and evidence obligations until exercised; the
benchmark itself does not satisfy them.
