# Sound Seekers Curriculum-to-Mechanic Matrix

## Purpose

Movement is not evidence by itself. A jump, chase, collection or delivery only advances mastery when the child's action demonstrates a specific phonics decision. `src/data/questMechanicMatrix.js` is the executable contract and covers every target kind and every learning shell used by the forty-stop sequence.

| Learning evidence | Suitable world action | Credit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grapheme recognition | Find, collect, route, match | Chooses the requested written grapheme independently |
| Phoneme discrimination | Jump, catch, tune, sort | Acts on the object carrying the heard target sound |
| Blending | Build, sequence, connect | Orders sound parts and reads the resulting word |
| Segmenting | Place, assemble, forge | Maps every spoken phoneme to the correct part |
| Word reading | Deliver, feed, navigate | Reads the word before acting on its meaning |
| Sound sorting | Herd, route, match | Sends contrasting words to the correct sound group |
| Descriptor comprehension | Collect, rescue, deliver | Identifies the object from size, colour, number or noun phrase |
| Heart-word recall | Feed, sign route, story choice | Reads the irregular word without decoding prompts |
| Story comprehension | Navigate, choose, perform | Makes a route or action choice supported by the text |
| Morphology | Assemble, transform, connect | Builds and explains the changed word form |

## Mastery contract

- Recognition requires three independent correct responses from four attempts with no more than a light prompt.
- Transfer requires two independent correct responses across at least two different words.
- A target is not mastered from one repeated object or one lucky route choice.
- Boss stops require evidence from three domains and cumulative review.
- Wrong choices produce immediate physical feedback, then a reduced prompt before the child retries.

## Difficulty progression

1. One target and visually distinct distractors.
2. Same-category distractors with contrastive sounds or spellings.
3. The target embedded in a decodable word and a moving world object.
4. A short remembered instruction, route choice or multi-part sequence.
5. Spaced retrieval in a later chapter using a different physical action.
