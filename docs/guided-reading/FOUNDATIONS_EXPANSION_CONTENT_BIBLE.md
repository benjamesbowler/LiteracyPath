## Manuscript and teaching contract

Use natural U.S. English, concrete referents, varied premises, useful repetition,
clear cause and effect, earned endings and occasional action-based humor.
Values emerge through helpful action, patience, care, honesty or repair.
Do not append a moral or treat a character's ordinary identity as a defect.
Keep the content suitable for children and ESL learners and free of explicit
sexual material or overt political advocacy.

Every fiction manuscript needs a concrete goal, obstacle, genuine attempt,
observed turn and outcome. Nonfiction needs a worthwhile question and an
organizing relationship that develops toward a supported synthesis. Verify
material factual claims against primary sources and retain source references.
Avoid duplicating prior plots with only renamed props. In particular, the
first Small Steps sequence already contains crowded mats, wrong-size boxes,
cleaning spills, missing objects, bag zippers and wet/jam marks on Dad.

Keep the Story Bible's A/B/C print contracts: A has one line and at most six
words per page, B at most two lines/eight words per line/fourteen per page,
standard C at most three lines/twenty-two words per page. Use present tense for
A/B. Respect the existing extended and shared-reader contracts where those
profiles are justified. Short books must still resolve a complete micro-arc.

Each book has a brief teacher sequence grounded in its actual printed words:

1. **Sound–letter link:** a visible grapheme, its reviewed sound and a real word
   from the book; model listening for the sound and locating its spelling.
2. **Decode:** two or three actual words with accurate blending or word-part
   support; teach actual high-frequency exceptions explicitly. Leveled-book
   practice words do not imply that every word in the book is decodable.
3. **Connected reading:** let the child try the print, provide support as needed,
   reread for fluency and meaning, then use one specific retelling/explanation
   prompt. A daily-reading suggestion is a teaching routine, not an app mastery
   award. Record independent reading separately from narration and word help.

Add concise ESL meaning support where a decodable word is unfamiliar. Avoid
asking children to guess words from pictures. Teacher guidance stays outside
the child full-screen story and does not add scored quizzes.

Prerequisites accumulate in that order and must be shown in the teacher guide
and summarized beside the cycle label. The child still receives supported
practice until actual independent evidence warrants another claim. Each new
word requires exact reviewed pronunciation. A final written s may represent
the already taught /s/ in a declared word shape, such as *taps*; this does not
license /z/, other endings, silent letters or arbitrary morphology. At Cycle 24,
reviewed *sniffs* retains the taught `ff` → /f/ before final `s` → /s/; this
specific /fs/ ending is permitted with the final-consonant lesson. It does not
move any other final spelling chunk into the middle of a word. No new
vowel sound, vowel team, split digraph, `ck`, contraction or untaught spelling
is introduced by these blending lessons. Earlier books and the original Small
Steps sequence retain their narrower scope.
