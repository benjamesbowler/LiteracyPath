# Child and teacher pilot protocol

## 1. Preconditions

Do not begin child sessions until:

- local legal/ethics, school and safeguarding routes approve the protocol;
- expert-review critical findings are closed or the affected content is removed;
- school permission and parent/guardian consent are recorded;
- the child gives current assent;
- the release/content version and item forms are frozen;
- an adult-only rehearsal proves event capture, offline recovery and export;
- the facilitator knows the stop, incident and withdrawal procedure.

Participation is voluntary. School access, teaching, grades, services and rewards
must not depend on taking part.

## 2. Session schedule

| Session | Timing | Maximum planned activity | Required outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Familiarisation | Before pre, if needed | 5 minutes; neutral navigation only | Device/accessibility setup; no target teaching |
| Pre | Day 0 | 10–15 minutes | Baseline items, latency, support use, deviations |
| Practice | Pre-registered schedule | Short age-appropriate blocks | Exposure dose, activities, completion, support |
| Immediate post | Within 0–2 days of final practice | 10–15 minutes | Parallel items and usability tasks |
| Retention | 7–21 days after post | 10–15 minutes | Parallel retention and unseen transfer |
| Teacher workflow | Before and after classroom use | 30–45 minutes | Task outcomes, errors, interpretation and feedback |

Use shorter blocks or stop earlier when the child’s wellbeing, attention or
access needs require it. Record the deviation; do not pressure completion.

## 3. Child assent script

Read in calm, age-appropriate language:

> We are trying some reading activities to learn whether the activities are
> clear and helpful. This is not a test of you. You can say yes or no. You can
> take a break or stop at any time. Nothing bad will happen if you stop. I may
> watch where the activity is easy or confusing, but I will not put your name
> in the research file. Would you like to try it?

Ask the child to explain or show how they can stop. Record `assented` only after
an affirmative response. Check assent again at every session and whenever the
child shows reluctance.

## 4. Standard facilitator behavior

The facilitator may:

- repeat the approved administration instruction;
- help with device access without teaching the answer;
- invoke approved accessibility adjustments;
- offer a break or stop;
- use the product’s declared support ladder when the protocol allows it.

The facilitator may not:

- praise correctness differently across phases;
- teach the target during pre/post/retention/transfer;
- point to a picture or context clue to elicit a word;
- reveal an answer, sound or spelling outside the declared support;
- turn `no_response` into incorrect;
- continue after assent is withdrawn;
- edit or remove a valid unfavorable record.

Record every unplanned prompt with time, task and reason.

## 5. Pre, post and retention administration

For each item:

1. Confirm the correct participant code, session, phase and frozen version.
2. Present the complete standard instruction.
3. Start latency when the complete stimulus becomes available.
4. Record the first committed response and response status.
5. Record every support stage separately.
6. Stop latency during interruptions or device failures.
7. Do not reveal correctness unless the frozen phase protocol explicitly allows
   feedback.
8. Record discontinuation, not administration and no response distinctly.

Forms must be parallel, not identical. Counterbalance forms when practical and
pre-register the allocation. Transfer items must pass the non-exposure check in
the measurement plan and exporter.

## 6. Area 2 child-usability observation

Run these tasks in order on first use, then repeat the starred tasks after
familiar use:

| Task ID | Child goal | Success without interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| U-LOGIN | Enter through the child login | Reaches own child home without entering teacher/admin space |
| U-NEXT* | Decide what to do next | Identifies and activates the dominant continuation action |
| U-RESUME* | Resume assigned work | Returns to the intended route and position |
| U-HELP* | Get help on a reading word | Uses whole word, sounds and reread stages intentionally |
| U-RECOVER | Recover from staged offline/save message | Finds the offered safe recovery without losing visible work |
| U-COMPLETE* | Complete and return | Understands completion and returns to a meaningful next state |
| U-EXPLAIN | Explain the next step | Gives a child-language description consistent with the UI |

For each task record:

- `independent`, `completed_with_prompt`, `not_completed` or `stopped`;
- duration in seconds;
- navigation/error count;
- adult-prompt count and exact approved prompt code;
- observed confusion code;
- accessibility barrier code;
- recovery success;
- child affect: comfortable, neutral, frustrated, distressed or not observed.

Do not infer emotion from a screenshot. Distressed ends the task and triggers
the wellbeing procedure.

## 7. Teacher workflow protocol

Give the teacher a realistic but non-production pilot class and these goals,
without describing click paths:

1. Find a learner needing attention and explain the evidence basis.
2. Start the intended assessment and correct a final-item mis-tap.
3. Find Guided Reading support-use events for one word.
4. Distinguish direct assessment, practice and teacher observation.
5. Export a scoped report and explain its date range and missing evidence.
6. Recover from a staged offline/failed-save state.
7. Create or update an instructional group and identify its next action.

Record task state, duration, errors, help requests, misinterpretations,
confidence rating (1–5), workload rating (1–5) and redacted feedback. Do not
train the teacher during the first attempt; offer a standardized help card only
after the help request is recorded.

## 8. Safety, accessibility and incidents

Stop immediately for:

- child withdrawal, distress or repeated reluctance;
- safeguarding disclosure or concern;
- exposure of another child’s identity or records;
- incorrect/harmful instructional modeling;
- inaccessible task with no approved equivalent;
- device behavior that risks evidence loss or privacy.

Follow the school safeguarding route for disclosures. Do not copy disclosure
details into the research dataset. Record only incident code, time, escalation
route and whether the session stopped. The study owner determines notification,
containment and deletion with the local authority/data controller.

Reasonable adjustments are recorded before the task where possible. Report
outcomes with the adjustment; do not present adjusted and unadjusted scores as
equivalent without expert justification.

## 9. Deviations and data quality

Every deviation receives:

- deviation ID;
- participant/session/task codes;
- date/time;
- planned procedure;
- actual procedure;
- reason;
- affected outcomes;
- keep/exclude decision made from the pre-declared rule;
- owner and review date.

Daily quality checks verify consent/assent, participant/session linkage, device
clock, phase, item uniqueness, latency bounds, support-stage values and missing
reasons. Corrections append an audit entry; raw records are not overwritten.

## 10. End-of-session script

Say:

> Thank you. You helped us see what was clear and what was confusing. You did
> not have to get everything right. Is there anything you want to tell me, and
> do you still feel okay about us using what we recorded today?

Record continuing assent. Remind the child and guardian how to withdraw. Do not
promise a reward or product change.

## 11. Pilot completion

The pilot is complete only when recruitment flow, withdrawals, missingness,
deviations, incidents, version history, descriptive outcomes, suppressed cells,
teacher observations, child usability findings and revision dispositions are
reported. Absence of a recorded incident is not assumed; the owner must record
an accurate zero-incidents statement or list incidents.
