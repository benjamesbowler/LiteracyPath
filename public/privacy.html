Privacy notice

Effective and last reviewed: 21 August 2026

For children: your teacher gives you a first-name or nickname account. Literacy Guide remembers your reading work so your teacher can help you. It does not show adverts, sell your information or ask you to talk to strangers. You can ask your teacher or a trusted adult if you want to know what is saved or want something corrected.

1. Who is responsible

Literacy Guide is operated by Benjamin Bowler, trading as Literacy Guide. Privacy contact: benjamesbowler@gmail.com.

For school-directed learning, the school normally decides why learner information is used and is the controller. Literacy Guide acts as its processor when it follows those instructions. Literacy Guide is a controller for its own teacher-account security, service protection, legal records and direct website request logs. The real activity—not a label in a contract—decides the role.

2. The two current routes

School-authorised service

A teacher account creates classes and child profiles. Children sign in using a class code and three-picture credential. The service stores learning records so teachers can teach, assess and report.

Anonymous try-out

The try-out creates no account, accepts no name, replaces application storage with temporary memory, disables database and authentication calls, and discards the session when the page closes. The hosting provider still receives ordinary request metadata needed to deliver the page, such as IP address, user agent, requested URL and time.

3. Information used

  • Teachers: email, display name, school, account status, classes, settings, service activity, email-confirmation state and versioned legal acceptance.
  • Children: first name or chosen display name, class, picture-code credential material, accessibility/support settings, answers, progress, reading records, assessment evidence, reports, interventions and reward/game state.
  • Device storage: essential sign-in state, settings, cached content, local progress, reports and retry queues needed for offline reliability and safe recovery.
  • Security and reliability: time, release version, event type, one-way fingerprints, sync counts and redacted error details. The error pipeline is designed to exclude names, answers, tokens, full URLs and school, class or learner identifiers.
  • Hosting requests: IP address, user agent, URL, time and related security/delivery logs held by Vercel and network providers.

Literacy Guide does not ask for a child’s email, surname, home address, phone number, precise location, uploaded photograph or free-form health/safeguarding notes. Schools must not add information that is not needed.

5. What Literacy Guide does not do

  • No advertising, behavioural tracking or sale/rental of personal data.
  • No direct messages, public profiles, child-to-child chat or stranger contact.
  • No use of identifiable learner data to train an AI model.
  • No learner prompts, answers or profiles sent to an AI provider in the deployed application.
  • No optional analytics or marketing cookies.

6. Providers and international transfers

  • Supabase supplies authentication, PostgreSQL storage, database functions, backups and platform support. The linked production project is currently in Sydney, Australia (ap-southeast-2).
  • Vercel hosts and delivers the site and processes service-generated request/security data through its global network; its current DPA states that primary processing facilities are in the United States.
  • Self-hosted fonts and media load from Literacy Guide. Google Fonts, advertising and analytics services are not used by the application.

These locations can involve a restricted transfer from the UK. Before onboarding a UK school with real learner data, the parties must verify the provider contract and appropriate safeguard (such as an applicable UK addendum/IDTA), complete the current ICO data-protection test, record the transfer chain and confirm the school accepts the storage location. A provider’s published document is not treated as proof that the operator’s account has completed the required contracting step.

7. Retention and deletion

The school controls its instructional retention within the product’s safeguards. Current default settings flag inactive learners after 365 days, delete archived learner records after 90 days, target provider deletion evidence within 30 days and backup expiry evidence within 35 days. A school can set permitted alternatives and choose archive or delete at year end.

Active learner deletion uses a staged, verified workflow covering database and browser copies. Teacher accounts and legal/security records are kept only while needed for the beta, the school relationship, security, legal claims or statutory duties. Anonymous try-out activity is not stored by Literacy Guide, although provider request logs follow the provider’s configured retention.

8. Rights and complaints

UK data-protection rights can include being informed, access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection, portability and safeguards around automated decisions. They depend on the basis and circumstances. Children have these rights too and may exercise them when they understand the request.

For a school learner record, contact the school first or email Literacy Guide. Identity and authority will be checked and the request routed safely. Rights requests are normally answered within one month. To make a privacy complaint, email benjamesbowler@gmail.com with “privacy complaint” in the subject. We will acknowledge it within 30 days, investigate without undue delay, keep you informed and explain the outcome.

If you remain unhappy, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office. You do not have to give up any court or statutory right.

9. Children, safety and changes

Settings are privacy-protective by default, only the minimum learner identity is requested, and school adults manage access. A school must complete its own DPIA where processing is likely to be high risk and assess the Children’s Code, safeguarding, lawful basis, accessibility and procurement requirements that apply to its deployment.

Material changes to purposes, data, providers, storage locations, AI use, analytics or child access require a new dated notice and product review. We will not quietly use existing learner information for an unrelated purpose.
